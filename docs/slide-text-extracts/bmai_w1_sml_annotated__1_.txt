Empirical risk minimization (ERM)


Suppose that a phenomenon of interest (e.g. house prices) is governed by an unknown mathematical
function 𝑓 ∗ that takes as input a list 𝑥 of features (e.g. house measurements) and outputs a target 𝑦
(e.g. house price).
Furthermore, suppose that
                                                𝑦 = 𝑓 ∗ 𝑥 + 𝜖,
where 𝜖 is a random value that on average is 0. More precisely, 𝜖 ∼ 𝒩 0, 𝜎 2 , with unknown 𝜎 2 .


We don’t have access to 𝑓 ∗ . Instead, we have only a set of observations 𝑥1 , 𝑦1 , 𝑥2 , 𝑦2 , … , 𝑥𝑛 , 𝑦𝑛 . How
can we build an estimator 𝑓መ of 𝑓 ∗ ?




              D-INFK Lehre                                                                          19.02.2026   28
Empirical risk minimization (ERM)


Vapnik proposed empirical risk minimization


1. Collect observations: 𝑥1 , 𝑦1 , 𝑥2 , 𝑦2 , … , 𝑥𝑛 , 𝑦𝑛 .
2. Propose a model ℋ comprising of all mathematical functions of a particular form. Examples:
   1. Linear functions: 𝑦𝑖 = 𝑓 𝑥𝑖 = 𝑤 𝑥𝑖 + 𝑏.
                                                                           𝑗
  2. Polynomial functions: 𝑦𝑖 = 𝑓 𝑥𝑖 = 𝑤0 + 𝑤1 𝑥𝑖 + 𝑤2 𝑥𝑖2 + ⋯ + 𝑤𝑗 𝑥𝑖 + ⋯
  3. …
3. Compute the empirical risk minimizer
                                        𝑓መ = 𝑎𝑟𝑔𝑚𝑖𝑛𝑓∈ℋ ෍ ℒ 𝑦𝑖 , 𝑓 𝑥𝑖   .
                                                             𝑖≤𝑛

Here, ℒ is a function that measures differences between targets and estimates.
Vapnik elaborates in his book why this is the most reasonable approach. As of today, this remains the best
strategy for estimating dependencies from data!

Robbins-Monro algorithm


Given 𝑥1 and 𝑦1 , find 𝑤 such that 𝑦1 − 𝑤𝑥1 = 0. That is, such that 𝑦1 = 𝑤𝑥1 .
1. Make a guess 𝑤
                ෥
2. As long 𝑦1 − ෥ 𝑤𝑥1 is unacceptably large, repeat the following
   1. If 𝑦1 < ෥
              𝑤𝑥1 , then decrease ෥
                                  𝑤.
   2. If 𝑦1 > ෥
              𝑤𝑥1 , then increase ෥
                                  𝑤.

A bit more advanced example


• Suppose we observe only one house with area 𝑥1 and price 𝑦1 .
• We hypothesize that 𝑦 = 𝑤𝑥 + 𝑏 for some unknown 𝑤 and 𝑏. Mathematically, this means that we
  choose ℋ as the set of all linear functions.
• We can define ℒ 𝑦, 𝑦ො = |𝑦 − ො
                               𝑦|.
• Our mission is then to find
                                𝑓መ = 𝑎𝑟𝑔𝑚𝑖𝑛𝑓∈ℋ ℒ 𝑦1 , 𝑓 𝑥1   = 𝑦1 − 𝑤𝑥1 − 𝑏 .
• This can be solved using the Robbins-Monro algorithm! The loop alternates between updating 𝑤 and 𝑏.

How about multiple houses?


• In practice, we have many houses available: 𝑥1 , 𝑦1 , 𝑥2 , 𝑦2 , … , 𝑥𝑛 , 𝑦𝑛 .
• Our goal then becomes
                              𝑓መ = 𝑎𝑟𝑔𝑚𝑖𝑛𝑓∈ℋ ෍ ℒ 𝑦𝑖 , 𝑓 𝑥𝑖    = ෍ |𝑦𝑖 − 𝑤𝑥𝑖 − 𝑏| .
                                             𝑖≤𝑛                𝑖≤𝑛

• This can be solved using the alternating Robbins-Monro algorithm!

What if we also have the area, number of rooms, age, and other features
of a house?

• In practice, the measurement 𝑥𝑖 = (𝑥𝑖1 , 𝑥𝑖2 , … , 𝑥𝑖𝑑 ) is a vector.
• Our hypothesis changes to 𝑦𝑖 = 𝑤1 𝑥𝑖1 + 𝑤2 𝑥𝑖2 + ⋯ + 𝑤𝑑 𝑥𝑖𝑑 + 𝑏 = 𝑤 ⊤ 𝑥 + 𝑏 for some unknown
  𝑤 = (𝑤1 , … , 𝑤𝑑 ) and 𝑏.
• Our goal then becomes
                              𝑓መ = 𝑎𝑟𝑔𝑚𝑖𝑛𝑓∈ℋ ෍ ℒ 𝑦𝑖 , 𝑓 𝑥𝑖      = ෍ |𝑦𝑖 − 𝑤 ⊤ 𝑥 − 𝑏| .
                                               𝑖≤𝑛                 𝑖≤𝑛

• This can be solved using the altenating Robbins-Monro algorithm!

Use gradients


• The Robbins-Monro update rule uses only the difference between target and estimation.
• A more efficient update rule uses the gradient of the loss function.




              D-INFK Lehre                                                                19.02.2026   38
Use neural networks instead of linear models


• Neural networks are universal approximators. They can approximate arbitrarily well any continuous
  function on a compact domain!
Use batches instead of the entire dataset


• At each iteration of the update loop, don’t recompute the loss on the entire data; use only a “batch” (i.e.,
  a subset of the dataset)
• Integrating all these improvements yields stochastic gradient descent, one of the most powerful
  modern algorithms for estimating dependencies on data.
