## Context

The house convention for BMAI coding exercises is a Colab notebook with a student version and a
solutions version kept in sync. `exercise/` already ships that pair for Heron alone, and it works
today.

The constraint pushing the other way is time. The master schedule reads 10:00 CX and 10:30 coffee,
which is thirty minutes for three exercises. Opening Colab, signing in and running a setup cell costs
a few minutes, and paying that three times inside thirty minutes spends a large fraction of the block
on boot. That cost is an estimate from the shape of the FS26 setup cells rather than a timed
measurement, and it should be timed before this issue is committed to.

The sibling exercise `hs26-bmai-w1-opt-rm-house` has already solved the hard part of the mechanics:
its notebooks are **generated** by `precompute/build_notebooks.py`, which asserts the student and
solutions sync rule by construction. This exercise's `exercise/build_notebooks.py` does the same. A
merged notebook has to keep that property, which means one builder emitting one notebook pair from
three part-modules rather than three builders and a manual paste.

## Deliverables

### 1. A decision record, first

Before any code: a short section in `../CLAUDE.md` under the open decisions, recording what was
decided, by whom, on what date, and what the timing measurement was. A merge done without that
record cannot be undone by anyone who was not in the conversation.

### 2. The merged builder

One script that emits `hs26-bmai-w1-opt.ipynb` and `hs26-bmai-w1-opt_solutions.ipynb`, structured:

| Cell block | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | Title markdown: the three parts, who owns each, and the fictitious-data disclaimer for Part 3 |
| 2 | One `# @title` setup cell: imports, any pip install, the seed, and the helper functions all three parts share |
| 3 | Markdown `## Part 1: Heron` and this exercise's cells, unchanged in content |
| 4 | Markdown `## Part 2: The genie` |
| 5 | Markdown `## Part 3: House prices` |
| 6 | A closing `### What's next?` cell pointing at the 11:00 lecture |

Each part keeps its own sanity-check cell, so a participant who breaks Part 1 finds out inside Part 1.

### 3. The sync rule, preserved

The two emitted notebooks must have the same cell count and differ only in the bodies of the task
cells. Banner comments, hint comments and print statements stay byte identical. The builder asserts
this and exits nonzero rather than writing a drifted pair. `exercise/build_notebooks.py` already
contains that assertion; carry it forward rather than rewriting it.

### 4. What happens to the three per-exercise pairs

Pick one and record it:

- **Delete them**, and let the merged notebook be the only one. Simplest, and it means a TA who wants
  to run only Heron has to scroll.
- **Keep them as the per-exercise fallback**, generated by the same builder from the same part
  modules so they cannot drift. More machinery, and it survives one TA team dropping out of the
  merge.

The second is recommended, because the three exercises have three different owners and a shared
artifact with three owners is the thing most likely to rot.

### 5. The setup cell has to work three times

If a participant runs Part 3 without having run Part 1, nothing may break. Test this explicitly:
restart the runtime, run the setup cell, then run Part 3 alone.

## Implementation Notes

- Time the Colab boot before deciding. Three data points on the cohort's own laptops beats an
  argument.
- The three exercises use two different sign conventions: Heron and the house exercise write the
  residual as target minus estimate, and the genie writes it the other way round. Inside one notebook

  merged notebook has to name out loud in a markdown cell rather than let a participant discover
  between two code cells. See open question 3.
