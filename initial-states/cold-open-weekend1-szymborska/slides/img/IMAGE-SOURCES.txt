  The house rule that makes this file necessary: FILENAMES LIE. Open and look at
  every candidate before you promise it, and record "opened and inspected before
  use" here once that is actually true.

  Rights rule for this course: Switzerland is 70 years post mortem auctoris.
  A pre-1900 engraving is free by age; a modern SCAN of it may still carry a
  licence, and a 20th century photograph usually carries one.

  THIS DECK IS THE FIRST IN THE FAMILY WHOSE PLATES ARE NOT ALL PUBLIC DOMAIN.

  ATTRIBUTION IS A CONDITION HERE, the way it is for the Wellcome Whewell, and
  it is not a courtesy. It is met in two places: frame 1's caption prints
  "Wislawa Szymborska, Krakow, 2009. Mariusz Kubik, CC BY 3.0" and the
  references frame repeats it with both licences. Do not shorten either.

  SIZE NOTE, and this is why the plate is 3.6cm here where the other three are
  4.0cm. The file is only 360 x 480, which is the largest size Commons holds.
  At 3.6cm high that is 480 px over 1.42 inches, about 340 pixels per inch,
  comfortably above what a projector resolves. 4.0cm would still be fine at
  about 305 ppi, and the smaller size was chosen for layout: the frame carries
  two quotations in two languages and needed the vertical room.
  DO NOT UPSCALE THE FILE.

  WHY THIS ONE AND NOT A NOBEL PORTRAIT. nobelprize.org carries a photo gallery
  for the 1996 prize, and those images belong to the Nobel Foundation and are
  not free. Szymborska died on 1 February 2012, so a photograph of her is
  protected in Switzerland until the end of 2082 unless its photographer has
  licensed it. Kubik has, which is what makes this file usable at all.

  THE RIGHTS QUESTION ON THIS ONE, and it is the only real one in the deck.
  "No known copyright restrictions" is a statement by the holding institution
  that it is not aware of restrictions. It is NOT a licence and it is NOT a
  public domain determination. Popper died in 1994; a photograph taken about
  1980 by an unnamed photographer working for the LSE is very unlikely to be out
  of copyright in Switzerland. Three options, and the choice is Carlos's:
    1. Use it, credited as on frame 1, relying on the LSE statement.
    2. Ask the LSE Library directly. They answer this kind of question.
    3. Run frame 1 with no plate at all. The frame works without a face: cut the
       left column and let the two placement lines and the sentence hold it.
  Whatever is chosen, record the decision on the Status line above.

  Size note. At 600 x 769 the file is small. At 4.0cm high it is about 3.1cm
  wide and roughly 100 pixels per centimetre, which is thin for projection but
  acceptable for a plate this size. Do not upscale it.
