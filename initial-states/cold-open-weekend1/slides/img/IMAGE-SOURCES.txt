Images for the CAS BMAI HS26 weekend-1 cold open ("The Four Precepts").

READ THIS FIRST
  This directory is EMPTY on purpose. Nothing was downloaded during the build,
  and no stand-in image was invented. The deck compiles and projects as it
  stands: \plateh in ../beamer-preamble.tex draws a dashed box naming the
  missing file wherever a plate belongs, so the gap is visible rather than
  silent. Drop the file in with the exact name below and recompile twice.

  The house rule that makes this file necessary: FILENAMES LIE. Open and look at
  every candidate before you promise it, and record "opened and inspected before
  use" here once that is actually true. Commons carries near-identical names for
  different objects, different crops and different derivative works.

  Rights rule for this course: Switzerland is 70 years post mortem auctoris.
  A pre-1900 painting is free; the photograph OF it may not be, and a modern
  translation of an old text usually is not.

  TRAP, recorded during the proposal pass and NOT resolved. Commons carries at
  least six variants under similar names, including a Statens Museum for Kunst
  version, a "cleaned up" version and one labelled "digitally modified
  derivative". The attribution history of the Hals Descartes portraits is
  contested in the art literature. So:
    1. Open the file you download and look at it before you use it.
    2. Prefer the plain, unmodified scan over anything labelled cleaned up,
       retouched or derivative.
    3. The slide caption reads "Frans Hals, Rene Descartes (1649)" and makes no
       claim about which museum holds which canvas. Keep it that way. Do not
       "improve" the caption with a collection name without re-checking the
       Commons file, and do not name a museum at all if the variants disagree.
    4. Record the exact Commons file name and the download date on the "Status"
       line above once it is done, replacing the three NOTs.

  Aspect ratio note. At 2178 x 2958 the portrait is 0.736 wide for 1 high, so at
  height 4.1cm the real plate is about 3.0cm wide. The placeholder box currently
  drawn is a little wider than that, because it has to hold the filename, so the
  first frame shifts slightly when the real image lands. Re-render frame 1 and
  look at it after you drop the file in.

  the precedent it is built on carries no plate on that frame either: the AINIT
  Faust opening puts Rembrandt on frame 1 and Stieler on frame 2 and gives its

  (/Users/carloscotrini/Documents/git_ainit/rl-arcade/slides/rl-slides.tex,

IMAGES CONSIDERED AND NOT USED

  No portrait of Heron of Alexandria exists, and the Metrica has no Commons
  image the proposal pass could find; the Heron candidate would have projected a
  page of the 1903 Teubner scan instead. Most Commons images filed under "Zeno"
  show Zeno of Citium, the Stoic, three generations after Zeno of Elea. The
  Commons record for the "Blind monks examining an elephant" print gives an
  artist who died in 1724 and a date of 1888, which cannot both be right. All
  three belong to candidates this deck did not build; they are recorded here so
  the next person does not re-discover them. See
  ../../COLD-OPEN-CANDIDATES.md sections 2, 4 and 10.

  If the 16:00 lecture later adds the Hume frame that section 8 of the menu
  recommends, it needs one more plate: Allan Ramsay, "David Hume, 1711 to 1776.
  Historian and philosopher" (1766), public domain, 3365 x 4001 pixels,
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AAllan_Ramsay_-_David_Hume%2C_1711_-_1776._Historian_and_philosopher_-_Google_Art_Project.jpg
  The National Galleries of Scotland hold the painting and Commons carries
  several scans under near-identical names with different accession numbers
  (PG 1057, PG 3521). Pick one, record its accession number here, and open it
  before promising it.
