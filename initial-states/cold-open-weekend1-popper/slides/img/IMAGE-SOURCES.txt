  THE RIGHTS QUESTION ON THIS ONE, and it is the only real one in the deck.
  "No known copyright restrictions" is a statement by the holding institution
  that it is not aware of restrictions. It is NOT a licence and it is NOT a
  public domain determination. Popper died in 1994; a photograph taken about
  1980 by an unnamed photographer working for the LSE is very unlikely to be out
  of copyright in Switzerland. Three options, and the choice is Carlos's:
    1. Use it, credited as on frame 1, relying on the LSE statement.
    2. Ask the LSE Library directly. They answer this kind of question.
    3. Run frame 1 with no plate at all. The frame works without a face: cut the
       left column and let the two placement lines and the sentence hold it.
  Whatever is chosen, record the decision on the Status line above.

  Size note. At 600 x 769 the file is small. At 4.0cm high it is about 3.1cm
  wide and roughly 100 pixels per centimetre, which is thin for projection but
  acceptable for a plate this size. Do not upscale it.

  ALTERNATIVE CHECKED AND REJECTED, 2026-09-01. A CC0 file exists,
  File:Portret van William Whewell, RP-F-2001-7-350-20.jpg from the
  Rijksmuseum at 5662 x 3936, which would remove the attribution condition

  albumen print on the left page and a page of printed biography on the right.
  Using it would need a heavy crop of a small, stained original. This is the
  "filenames lie" rule earning its place: the metadata says portrait, the file
  is a book. Do not re-propose it without opening it.

  ATTRIBUTION IS A CONDITION HERE, not a courtesy. CC BY 4.0 requires credit,
  which is why frame 4's caption reads "Wellcome Collection, CC BY 4.0" and why
  the references frame repeats it with the L number. Do not shorten either.

  The engraving itself is old enough to be free by age; the licence attaches to
  Wellcome's scan. If Carlos would rather avoid the condition entirely, look for
  a plain photograph of an oil portrait of Whewell in a Trinity College or
  National Portrait Gallery scan, and check that scan's own licence before
  promising it.
